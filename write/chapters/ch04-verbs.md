# Verbs, tense, and voice

## Core idea

Put the real action in a direct verb. Use active voice when you know the actor.
Keep tense simple.

## Preferred forms

Strict STE permits the infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past,
simple future, and specified past participles used as adjectives. Avoid stacked
auxiliaries and progressive constructions when a simple tense works.

## Apply it

- Procedure: start the sentence with an imperative verb.
- Description: use `actor + verb + object`.
- Unknown actor in a description: passive voice can be acceptable.
- Nominalized action: turn the action noun into a verb.
- Phrasal verb: identify its exact meaning and select a direct verb or rewrite.

## Failure modes

- Inventing an actor to eliminate passive voice.
- Changing time when simplifying tense.
- Replacing a domain action with a broader common verb.
- Treating every word ending in `-ing` as wrong. It can be a technical noun or
  modifier.

## Worked examples

Passive:

> The file is read by the parser.

Active:

> The parser reads the file.

Nominalized:

> Perform an analysis of the log.

Direct:

> Analyze the log.

Unknown actor:

```text
The certificate was revoked on July 1.
```

Keep the passive form if the source does not identify who revoked it.
